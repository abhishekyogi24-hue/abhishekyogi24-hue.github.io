CheapOair AI Chatbot

0→1 → Gen AI rearchitecture · Owned end-to-end at Fareportal

The chatbot behind post-booking flight support at cheapoair.com, live today — owned end-to-end from a rule-based launch through a full rearchitecture into a multi-agent Gen AI system, as customers increasingly expected free-form, ChatGPT-style conversation.

Why a chatbot

Call and email were the only support channels. ~60% of bookings are international relative to the US, making calls expensive due to roaming, while email was too slow for time-sensitive travel issues. Validated the need through customer surveys on preferred support channels, IVR feedback, and frontline agents' voice-of-customer input, then greenlit a chatbot for quick post-booking flight support.

Phase 1 — Dialogflow ES (rule-based NLP)

Launched at 20% automation across basic flows — booking and billing details — with anything transactional handed off to travel experts. Built on Google Dialogflow ES: each intent hand-defined with training phrases, matched to user input via keyword-based NLP, each carrying its own responses, CTAs, and sub-intents.

Scaled to 50% automation, 62% CSAT, and 60% engagement by onboarding self-serve flows for new bookings, cancellations, exchanges, schedule changes, add-ons, CCD, and CCV — coordinating with product and business owners on each process, then owning integration end-to-end and enabling agent teams to back the chatbot. Day-to-day: reviewing chat transcripts, running stakeholder syncs, and turning feedback into the next round of conversational improvements.

Why we moved to Gen AI

As ChatGPT normalized free-form chat, users increasingly typed casually and expected multi-turn, contextual conversation — exposing Dialogflow ES's ceiling as basic keyword-matching NLP: no multi-turn memory, no context carry-over, and frequent intent misclassification sending users down the wrong flow. As a stopgap on major flows (exchange, cancel, schedule change, add-ons), once intent was identified via CTA or booking source we locked the typing box to a fixed path — free typing mid-flow had been derailing users into the wrong flow, hurting both conversion and satisfaction. In parallel, we ran a POC on Dialogflow CX with Google's product team, reviewing architecture, webhook handling, and the migration path, but shelved it on cost and ROI grounds.

Phase 2 — Vertex AI multi-agent architecture

Built our own architecture on Google Vertex AI APIs instead (we already held the GCP billing relationship), aimed squarely at better intent classification and a more natural chat experience: an orchestrator agent routing each query, backed by specialized agents — Intent Classifier, Booking Details, Small Talk, FAQs, and Agent Summarization.

Functionality shipped:

  • Digression — resume the original flow after answering an interrupting side question
  • Flow switch — confirm with the user before jumping from one major intent to another
  • Flow queries — answer side questions inside a major flow, then resume where it left off
  • Global queries — answer any query upfront, regardless of the current flow
  • Clarification — surface likely next steps when a query is ambiguous

Offline eval

Golden sets (ground truth), adversarial sets (edge cases, prompt injection), and regression sets (basic sanity checks).

Online eval

LLM-as-judge plus App Insights logging, scored on groundedness, faithfulness, safety, jailbreak resistance, and answer relevancy.

Compliance & rollout

Partnered with Legal, InfoSec, and the internal AI committee to clear the system against Gen AI usage guidelines. Opened first to internal stakeholders, then org-wide, gathering and acting on feedback at each stage, before going live to 5% of production traffic.

Oli — AI Trip Planner

0→1 product strategy & PRD · CheapOair (Fareportal)

A conversational, AI-powered trip-planning assistant embedded in the CheapOair mobile app — owned from problem framing and PRD through a phased launch roadmap. The vision: collapse destination discovery, flight search, itinerary planning, and personalized guidance into one journey-aware conversational interface, and make Oli the primary way travelers plan.

Problem — planning travel is fragmented

Travelers discover destinations in one place, search flights in another, and research itineraries separately — returning multiple times before booking and losing all planning context between sessions. Worse, the app experience and Oli's conversations ran as two disconnected journeys, ignoring the contextual signals — searches, bookings, browsing behavior, location, preferences — that could make guidance feel personal. Origins had to be typed in manually, and prior context was near-impossible to reuse.

Who it's for — three planning mindsets

Explorer

"I don't know where to go." Wants inspiration, destination recommendations, and budget-driven ideas.

Planner

"I know where I want to go." Wants itinerary creation, recommendations, and comparison options.

Shopper

"I want the best flight." Wants flight discovery plus alternate-airport and alternate-date suggestions.

Core capabilities scoped

  • Destination discovery — describe preferences conversationally and get location- and origin-aware destination ideas
  • Trip planning — generate itineraries, ask trip questions, and refine plans through conversation
  • Flight discovery — search conversationally, with flight tiles that surface alternate dates and nearby airports
  • Context persistence — resume eligible past conversations so planning survives across sessions
  • Journey intelligence — read searches, browsing, booking status, intent, and trip stage to guide proactively
  • Proactive engagement & sharing — reach travelers via push, email, and in-app messaging; share destinations and itineraries

Personalization framework

Three signal layers feed the experience: geolocation (with consent — detect the current city, confirm it, and use it as a default origin), behavioral signals (searches, bookings, browsing, preferences, engagement history), and conversation memory (planning context, selected itineraries, and destination interests carried forward).

Phased roadmap

Phase 1

Destination discovery, itinerary generation, flight discovery, sharing, copy, and past conversations.

Phase 2

App-behavior syncing, journey intelligence, and personalized recommendations.

Phase 3

Proactive notifications, recommendation-engine optimization, and deep booking-funnel integration.

Arthritis Companion

0→1 · Designed, built, and shipped end-to-end

A login-free daily companion for tracking arthritis symptoms and gentle exercises — designed around one real person's arthritic hands, not a generic user persona.

Problem

Existing symptom trackers assume fine motor control: sliders, drag gestures, small tap targets, multi-step forms. For someone managing arthritis, the tool meant to help was itself hard to use.

Design constraints

Large tap targets, no sliders or drag gestures, voice-friendly notes, and a fixed bottom "Done" button reachable one-handed. No login, no account — every extra step is a barrier.

Approach

Local-first architecture: all data stored on-device via IndexedDB, no backend, exportable backups. Four focused screens — Today, History, Exercises, Guides — instead of one dense dashboard.

Outcome

A installable, offline-capable PWA that a real user opens daily to log symptoms and exercises in seconds, one-handed, with zero account friction.
